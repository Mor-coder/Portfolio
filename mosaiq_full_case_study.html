Full case study — designing trust in AI research workflows

Full case study

NDA-safe deep dive. This page walks through the key design explorations and shipped patterns that helped finance and market-research teams manage large datasets and trust AI outputs in high-stakes work.

The story is organised around three linked problems: collecting data at scale, synthesising it with AI without losing provenance, and turning insights into deliverables.

Context snapshot

  • Audience: finance consultants and researchers responsible for investment-grade outputs

  • Environment: large, messy datasets with changing scope and tight deadlines

  • Design focus: reduce cognitive load + make AI behaviour legible

Users & goals

Our primary users were finance consultants — highly capable at interpreting market signals, and accountable for producing clear recommendations under uncertainty. The product needed to support speed without compromising confidence.

Key needs we designed for

  • Collect and browse large volumes of content without losing context

  • Synthesize information quickly — and verify what the AI used to form an answer

  • Organise findings into repeatable deliverables (memos, reports, summaries)

Case study

Collecting and managing data at scale

Early on, the platform supported fewer file types, analysis was slower, and the assistant’s scope was mostly limited to uploaded files (not the web). As teams scaled their research, three issues appeared: uploading was inconsistent, browsing was slow, and selecting the right subset of files for analysis wasn’t obvious.

How might we…

  • Help users collect, find, and browse documents in as few steps as possible?

  • Make filtering and ordering fast without turning the UI into a control panel?

Before: limited file support and basic database browsing

After: broader file support + tabs and filters for faster skimming

Design decisions

Inspired by patterns from Finder and Gmail, the project page evolved into a “research hub”: clearer structure, quicker selection, and better feedback when uploads fail.

  • Added checkboxes for fast single/multi-select workflows

  • Introduced tabs to group content and reduce scanning effort

  • Placed filters above tabs for “skim and narrow” behaviour

  • Improved failure states for restricted or unparseable content (e.g., paywalled pages)

Browser extension for capturing multiple pages during early research

Users could add content in two ways:

  • From inside the project: use Add Content and choose a source type

  • From outside the project: use the extension to capture multiple pages quickly

Case study

Synthesising data — designing trust in AI outputs

As the assistant became more capable, the UX challenge shifted from “can it answer?” to “can users verify it?”. We supported three ways to retrieve insights while keeping provenance visible:

  • Automatic AI insights (summaries + key points)

  • Chat with the assistant (queries + follow-ups)

  • AI Modules (repeatable tasks for consistent outputs)

Automatic AI insights

Each file surfaced a fast “understand it now” layer: summary and key points. Cards could be copied or saved for later synthesis.

Saved cards lived in Saved Insights, nested under their source file so users could reopen context and keep provenance intact.

Asking the AI

Research doesn’t move in a straight line. Users needed to stay oriented (what’s selected, what’s being analysed) and verify outputs quickly — without breaking flow.

How might we…

  • Make it obvious which files the assistant is using — at all times?

  • Support natural follow-ups and tangents without losing the thread?

  • Help users trace answers back to sources and understand context?

Selected files switch to a darker background to strengthen the selected/unselected distinction. We also introduced “Reply to | 4 files” so users always knew what the assistant was analysing — especially helpful when switching contexts inside large databases.

We iterated on “reasoning visibility.” The Logic CTA became the right balance: available when needed, but not always visible (to avoid noise and preserve conversational flow).

Reasoning lives in the chat because it’s part of the interaction — not separate documentation.

To support focused follow-ups, we added a Reply interaction so users could ask about a specific segment of an answer without losing the thread.

As output quality increased, source verification became non-negotiable. Citations were expanded to support both text and non-text data — and tuned for “quick context”, not tiny excerpts.

  • Larger citation bubbles for more readable context

  • Support for charts, graphs, and tables

  • View in source to restore the full context of the claim

Case study

Turning research into deliverables

As MosaiQ matured, the workflow expanded beyond answering questions. Customers needed consistent, repeatable outputs: reports, memos, and stakeholder-ready summaries.

AI Modules

AI Modules are pre-configured tasks: a structured set of instructions that produce a consistent output (e.g., “X Report”). They reduced time spent rewriting prompts and improved repeatability across teams.

Case study

Templates

Templates provided a structured canvas for assembling deliverables. Users could add sections and fill them with saved insights and validated content — reducing the blank-page problem.

How might we…

  • Offer a flexible space where users can structure deliverables in a way that matches their thinking?

Templates became the main path for report building: add sections, pull in saved insights, and refine the final narrative into something publication-ready.

Exploration

AI Modules + Templates (concept)

We explored pairing Templates with Modules: each template section becomes a prompt linked to a module. When invoked, the deliverable keeps the template structure while sections are auto-populated by the AI.

This is an exploration and was not developed.

Exploration

Iterating on a flexible canvas

Another direction explored a modular “tiles” canvas to better match how people assemble narratives: move pieces, group themes, and evolve report structure as research changes.

Iterating on a "booking a hotel" experience

There are certain things, like booking a trip away, that, despite being something we are used to, can still feel daunting. Perhaps, and here I am also dipping into my personal experience, this might be due to the expectations that we put on the trip as a whole. This means, the anticipation of the flight, of the accomodation, on the food, and so on and so forth...

While the anticipation could be a great hype factor, it can also lead to anxieties, overthinking, overplanning, and ultimately forgetting to enjoy the moment.

With this project, I've reproduced the experience of booking a hotel in order to try to understand what are the pain points people find, and, hopefully, how to smooth them out to create a more enjoyable experience.

The Research Phase

To familiarise myself with the context of booking and hospitality websites, I run a competitive benchmarking, researching what other business were doing.

I looked at how they structured their website, what kind of informations they provided and where, how they showed their booking system.

Once I had familiarised myself with the environments, I wanted to gain a deeper understanding of the experience from the users’ perspective—where they encountered pain points, what challenges they faced, and how we might address them. To explore this further, I conducted a series of usability tests.

The usability tests revealed the following key findings:

  • As users have different ways to achieve tasks (i.e. using the calendar VS inserting dates) it would be good for the software to provide multiple options to satisfy everyone.

  • Reviews, photos, and maps are essentials.

  • Photos help create a hotel’s first impression and to a certain extent, it helps the person understand the size of a room.

  • It’s best to avoid long room descriptions as people might not be bothered to read it through and miss important information, which ultimately might end up in them feeling cheated on.

  • Rather, be brief and use a clear format, perhaps icons, to describe the hotel and room’s amnesties.

  • Important information, such as price, free cancellation and breakfast should be listed earlier on and should be visible.

  • Clear room descriptions.

  • Present add-on once the room has been selected otherwise it might feel pushy and shady.

  • Be clever about the add-on, don’t just present the standard ones.

  • Present a clear summary of the booking repeating all the important info.

  • Try to remember the basic pieces of information selected by the guests when they’re navigating the website back and forth looking for the perfect room.

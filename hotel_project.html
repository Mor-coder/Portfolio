Redesigning a Hotel Booking Journey to Reduce Friction and Build Trust

A concept UX case study focused on decision-making, clarity, and reassurance in high-stakes flows.

My role

UX/UI Designer (end-to-end)

Scope

Benchmarking, usability testing, synthesis, journey mapping, wireframes, prototyping

Project type

Concept redesign (hotel booking flow)

Summary

I redesigned a hotel booking journey to reduce decision friction and build trust at critical moments—particularly during room selection, policy review, and add-ons. Through usability testing and synthesis, I uncovered where hesitation quietly accumulated across the flow and translated those insights into clearer information hierarchy, more scannable content, and better-timed prompts that support confident decision-making.

Impact & outcomes

The redesigned flow reduced visible hesitation and improved decision confidence during usability testing. Participants moved through room comparison more decisively, asked fewer clarification questions about policies, and engaged more thoughtfully with add-ons once these were introduced later in the journey.

  • Smoother progression: Fewer backtracks and reduced scrolling during key decision points.

  • Higher perceived trust: Clear pricing and cancellation policies increased confidence in committing.

  • More deliberate upgrades: Add-ons felt helpful rather than pushy when introduced after room choice.

What changed

  • Clearer hierarchy surfaced price, cancellation, and breakfast options at the exact moments users needed them.

  • Room details were condensed into scannable summaries supported by visual cues and iconography.

  • Add-ons were repositioned after room selection to feel supportive rather than disruptive.

  • A reassurance-focused summary step helped users validate decisions before committing.

Impact & outcomes

While this was a concept project, usability testing and validation sessions indicated that the redesigned flow reduced moments of hesitation and made key decisions—such as comparing rooms, understanding policies, and evaluating add-ons—feel more straightforward and reassuring.

  • Clearer decision-making: Participants moved through room selection with fewer backtracks and clarification questions once pricing, cancellation terms, and breakfast options were surfaced earlier.

  • Lower cognitive load: Shorter descriptions, scannable layouts, and visual cues helped users process information faster and maintain momentum.

  • Improved trust signals: Reassurance patterns—such as a structured summary step and delayed add-ons—reduced anxiety near the point of confirmation.

What I’d do next

Given more time and engineering support, I would focus on stress-testing the redesigned flow at scale and exploring how personalisation could make decisions feel even lighter—particularly for returning users or complex trips.

  • Test progressive disclosure models for policies and add-ons to balance transparency with simplicity.

  • Experiment with comparison views for rooms to support faster trade-off decisions.

  • Explore behavioural nudges rooted in real data—such as demand signals or reminder patterns—while avoiding dark patterns.

  • Run A/B tests on summary-page layouts to quantify which reassurance elements most reduce abandonment.

What I learned

Looking back, the biggest takeaway was learning to treat user hesitation as data. Moments where people paused, scrolled excessively, or re-read details weren’t noise—they were signals. Designing for trust meant addressing those signals systematically, not cosmetically.

Reflection

This project reinforced how closely trust and decision-making are linked in complex flows. Small moments—hesitation, rereading policies, scrolling back to compare options—often reveal more than direct complaints. Learning to treat those micro-signals as design inputs rather than edge cases shaped how I now approach experience design more broadly.

It also deepened my appreciation for structured synthesis. Turning messy, qualitative research into clear design direction—without oversimplifying nuance—became one of the most rewarding parts of the process. The work reminded me that clarity is rarely about removing complexity entirely; it’s about revealing the right information at the right moment.

Ultimately, the project sharpened how I balance empathy with rigor: holding users’ emotional states in mind while grounding decisions in evidence, iteration, and practical constraints.

Full case study

NDA-safe deep dive. Below is a detailed walkthrough of the design explorations, iterations, and UI solutions developed throughout the project.

Understanding the user

Our target audience consisted of travellers aged 30–60 who value comfort and quality and are willing to spend more when the experience feels reliable and thoughtfully designed. They approach booking with a mix of excitement and caution: eager to plan, but sensitive to uncertainty around pricing, policies, and what is truly included.

Defining insights from data analysis:

  • Users seek reassurance early, especially around cancellation policies and total price

  • Long descriptions and dense layouts increase hesitation rather than confidence

  • Photos, reviews, and summaries play a critical role in shaping trust

research phase

To familiarise myself with hotel-booking patterns and hospitality platforms, I ran a competitive benchmarking exercise, analysing how other businesses structured their pages, surfaced critical information, and guided users through booking interactions. In parallel, I conducted a series of usability tests to understand the experience from the users’ perspective—where friction emerged, how uncertainty built over time, and which moments triggered hesitation or second-guessing.

The usability tests revealed four recurring themes across participants:

1. Flexibility & User Control

  • Users rely on different methods to complete tasks (e.g., typing dates vs. using a calendar), highlighting the need for multiple pathways to achieve the same action.

  • The system should retain the user’s previous selections during navigation to prevent unnecessary repetition.

2. Information Clarity & Hierarchy

  • Users expect essential information—such as price, free cancellation, breakfast availability—to be clearly visible early in the flow.

  • Long room descriptions discourage reading, risking missed details. Instead, users prefer concise, scannable descriptions.

  • Visual aids (e.g., icons) help simplify amenity and room information.

3. Visual Content & Decision Support

  • Photos, maps, and reviews are key decision-making tools and strongly influence trust.

  • Photos also help users understand the scale and layout of a room.

4. Add-ons & Transparency

  • Add-ons should appear after the room selection, as showing them earlier feels pushy or misleading.

  • Users respond better to thoughtful, relevant add-ons rather than generic options.

  • A clear summary page is essential for reassurance, helping users verify all important details before confirming.

synthesis & insights

To make sense of the research findings, I organised observations into an affinity diagram. This helped surface behavioural patterns, cluster pain points, and highlight design opportunities that could meaningfully improve the booking experience.

Key Insight Clusters:
1. User Flexibility & Control

  • Users approach tasks differently (e.g., typing dates vs. using a calendar).

  • Systems that remember user choices prevent frustration when navigating back and forth.

2. Clarity & Information Hierarchy

  • Essential information (price, free cancellation, breakfast) must be visible early.

  • Room descriptions should be concise and scannable.

  • Icons or visual cues improve comprehension of amenities and room features.

3. Visual Content & First Impression

  • Photos, reviews, and maps strongly influence trust and decision-making.

  • Visuals help users gauge room size and quality before booking.

4. Adds-on & Transparency

  • Add-ons should appear after the room is selected to avoid feeling pushy.

  • Presenting relevant, personalized add-ons enhances user satisfaction.

  • A clear booking summary reassures users and confirms choices.

journey mapping/experience gaps

Mapping the end-to-end journey helped me see how friction compounds over time rather than appearing in isolation. What I found fascinating was that, although the peak pain point surfaced at the “Add-ons” stage, frustration had already been building well before that. At the room-and-rates stage, uncertainty began to creep in: excessive scrolling, overly long descriptions, and key details hidden in secondary areas made users pause and re-evaluate their choices. By the time they reached add-ons, that hesitation had intensified. The flow felt cluttered, and trust had eroded—not because of a single issue, but because of repeated small moments of doubt. Even after completing the booking, some users described a lingering sense that something might still go wrong at the last minute.

ideation & early concepts

Early ideation focused on exploring multiple directions before committing to a single structure. I sketched variations around information density, layout rhythm, card-based selection, and how policies and add-ons could surface progressively rather than all at once. These explorations helped test how much information users needed at each step—and, just as importantly, when restraint was more effective than completeness.

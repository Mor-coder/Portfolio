MosaiQ Labs

Integrating AI into Research Workflows

Role

Product designer (UX/UI) - Junior

Scope

Core workflows, AI transparency patterns, reporting templates

Team

Design, Engineering, Sales

Timeline

2023 - 2025

Product

AI-powered research & reporting platform

Summary

I worked as a Junior UX/UI Designer on an AI-driven research platform used by finance and market-research professionals. My work focused on designing clear, trustworthy AI interactions that support complex decision-making, reduce cognitive load, and help users confidently move from research to insight in high-stakes workflows.

Skip to the full case study

Context

When I joined MosaiQ Labs in 2023, the company was building an AI-powered platform to support knowledge workers in managing and analysing large volumes of information. AI was transitioning from an experimental add-on to a core part of professional workflows, particularly in high-stakes domains like finance.

Beyond interface improvements, this work required balancing evolving AI capabilities, engineering feasibility, and user trust expectations. Design decisions were prioritised based on impact to research workflows and scalability across future AI modules.

Problem

Users needed to collect, synthesise, and validate large amounts of data while maintaining confidence in the accuracy and provenance of AI-generated insights. The core challenge was designing AI interactions that felt transparent, reliable, and easy to reason about, without overwhelming users or disrupting established research workflows.

Constraints

  • AI performance varied by file type and data source

  • Some sources failed to upload or parse due to restrictions

  • Large datasets increased cognitive load and risk of losing context

  • Engineering capacity required prioritising high-impact solutions

My Role & Responsibilities

I worked as a Junior UX/UI Designer embedded in a cross-functional team alongside engineers and the sales team. I contributed across the product lifecycle, from early problem framing and iteration to detailed UI design and handoff.

  • Designing end-to-end UX/UI for core platform features

  • Translating user feedback and business requirements into design solutions

  • Collaborating closely with engineers to ensure feasibility and quality

  • Iterating designs based on feedback and evolving AI capabilities

Key Design Challenges

  • Establishing trust in AI outputs for high-stakes decisions

  • Reducing cognitive load across large, evolving datasets

  • Making AI behaviour legible at all times

  • Supporting non-linear research flows

TL;DR

I helped design scalable data-management workflows, trust-building AI interactions, and flexible reporting tools that allowed users to move faster and feel more confident in high-stakes research and investment workflows.

  • Users reported smoother research flows and clearer organisation of large datasets

  • Teams described producing investment memos significantly faster

  • Improved trust in AI outputs through visible reasoning and citations

Selected UI outcomes

Before: limited file support and database navigation

After: tabs, filters, and broader file support for faster browsing

Trust-building: citations and transparency to verify AI outputs

Templates: moving from insights to structured deliverables

What I learned

Working on MosaiQ sharpened how I approach designing in technically complex, high-stakes environments where trust, speed, and clarity are essential. It pushed me to think rigorously about how users reason about information — especially when that information is generated by AI.

Designing AI isn’t just about interaction clarity; it’s about managing expectations between what the system can do, what users believe it can do, and what the business needs it to do.

  • Designing for trust: Making system behaviour visible through citations, selection states, and reasoning patterns dramatically changed how confident users felt relying on AI outputs.

  • Balancing ambition with feasibility: Close collaboration with engineers helped me learn how to propose bold ideas while shaping them into solutions that could realistically ship under technical constraints.

  • Working in ambiguity: Requirements evolved quickly as AI capabilities matured. I became comfortable reframing problems, testing directions early, and iterating in small, high-impact steps.

  • Human-first AI: I learned that good AI UX isn’t about adding power — it’s about reducing cognitive load, supporting non-linear thinking, and helping users feel in control of complex workflows.

Trade-offs & what didn’t work

Not every idea made it into the product as originally imagined. Designing in a fast-moving AI startup meant constantly testing assumptions, learning from real usage, and adjusting direction when early solutions didn’t produce the outcomes we hoped for.

Impact & outcomes

The work we shipped at MosaiQ materially improved how professionals collected information, reasoned about data, and produced investment-grade outputs using AI. My focus on clarity, trust, and workflow efficiency helped move the product from early experimentation to a mature research platform.

  • Stronger research workflows: Users reported smoother end-to-end flows from data collection to analysis, supported by clearer organisation and faster navigation across large databases.

  • Higher confidence in AI: Transparency patterns such as citations, file-selection cues, and on-demand reasoning reduced uncertainty and increased adoption of AI-generated insights.

  • Faster delivery of outputs: Qualitative feedback and customer interviews highlighted significant time savings when moving from exploration to investment memos and reports.

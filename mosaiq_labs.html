MosaiQ Labs

Integrating AI into research workflows

Role

Product designer (UX/UI) — Junior

Scope

Core workflows, AI transparency patterns, reporting templates

Team

Design, Engineering, Sales

Timeline

2023 – 2025

Product

AI-powered research & reporting platform

Summary

I worked on an AI-driven research platform used by finance and market-research professionals. My focus was designing clear, trustworthy AI interactions that support complex decision-making, reduce cognitive load, and help users move from research to insight with confidence.

What changed

  • Expanded project workflows and information architecture to support larger, messier datasets

  • Introduced AI transparency patterns (citations, reasoning, selection states) to make outputs verifiable

  • Improved navigation with tabs, filters, and clearer content grouping for faster browsing

  • Designed reporting templates to help users turn research into structured deliverables

Impact & outcomes

The features we shipped improved how users collected information, reasoned about it, and produced investment-grade outputs. My work focused on clarity, trust, and workflow efficiency as the platform matured from early experimentation to a reliable research tool.

  • Stronger research workflows: smoother organisation and faster navigation across large databases

  • Higher confidence in AI: transparency patterns reduced uncertainty and increased adoption

  • Faster delivery of outputs: teams reported producing memos and reports significantly faster

TL;DR

  • Designed scalable research workflows for collecting and organising large datasets

  • Built trust in AI outputs with citations, reasoning, and transparent interaction states

  • Improved browsing speed with clearer structure, tabs, and filters

  • Created templates that helped users turn insights into consistent deliverables

Selected UI outcomes
What I’d do next

If I continued evolving this platform, I’d double down on measurement, calibration, and explanation UX — so users can trust outputs while understanding when to verify, compare, or override.

  • Add instrumentation around “trust moments” (citation clicks, re-checking sources, edits to AI output)

  • Refine confidence cues (quality signals by file type/source and clear failure states)

  • Strengthen collaborative workflows (hand-offs, versioning, shared templates)

  • Expand guardrails for edge cases (partial parses, restricted docs, ambiguous sources)

What I learned

Designing AI isn’t only about interaction polish — it’s expectation management. The gap between what the system can do, what users think it can do, and what the business needs it to do is where trust is won or lost.

  • Trust is designed: visibility (citations, reasoning, states) changes behaviour

  • Feasibility matters early: working tightly with engineering shaped better decisions

  • Ambiguity is the job: AI capability shifts require iterative framing and fast learning loops

Reflection

This work taught me that “AI UX” is really research UX with higher stakes. When people can’t trace an answer back to a source, they don’t just feel confused — they feel exposed.

The most effective patterns weren’t flashy; they were the ones that helped users verify, stay oriented, and feel in control.
